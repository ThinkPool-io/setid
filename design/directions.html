Set Times

The festival clash-finder is the one printed document every raver studies: it maps time to who is playing. SetID makes that document for a single set. Listeners get the answer they came for in the shape they already read (a time, a block, a name); DJs get a set laid out as a running order they can scan for the stretch they want. Archivo is the only family, run through its width axis: condensed black capitals for names and headlines, expanded figures for every timestamp. Paper white, black rules, and four stage colours (tomato, sun, teal, ink blue) that rotate track by track, so colour always means one track. Home persuades with the timetable itself as proof; the set view keeps standard controls and lets the timetable rule and stage colours do the work.

Direction 2

Blend

sky #F7F8FAink #171A33indigo #2E3F9Emadder #B8323Fsaffron #EFA42A

A DJ set is not a list; it is tracks crossing each other. Blend draws the set the way it sounds: each track a translucent band on one of three lanes, and where two bands cross the colours mix into a third, which is exactly where a listener loses the thread and types "track ID?". That makes the hard moment the visible one for casual listeners, and gives DJs the transitions at a glance. Sun-white ground, translucent indigo, madder and saffron laid with multiply so overlaps compute their own chord colours. Unbounded, light and wide, for display and tracked lane labels; Hanken Grotesk for everything you read. The home is the canopy itself with the paste bar in the clearing; the set view keeps a plain layout and turns only the timeline into lanes.

Fonts
Unbounded (display, tracked labels) + Hanken Grotesk (UI and reading)
Palette
Committed translucency: sky white, indigo, madder, saffron, multiply-blended
Signature
Lane timeline where crossings are the transition windows (±20 s of the 40 s sampling)
Motion
Home bands drift slowly on independent clocks; still in the set view; off under reduced motion
Where it came from
A dealt challenger (a silk canopy of colour chords) that held on product clarity, so it stays a full direction.

Direction 3

Riso

fluoro pink #FF48B0navy #15204Ayellow #FFE800pink tint #FFC4E3

Before anyone posts "track ID?" under a video, they found the night through a flyer: two spot inks, a line-up stacked by importance, a tear-off strip at the bottom. Riso makes SetID that flyer. One fluorescent pink owns every surface; navy and yellow are the only other inks. The line-up is real data (track names sized by how long they played in the set), the paste bar is the tear-off ticket, and every cover prints as a pink duotone until you hover it, when it comes back in colour. Listeners get a page loud enough to share; DJs get covers and BPM chips on a dense, legible list. Anybody at its widest and heaviest for flyer type; Barlow Semi Condensed for the small print.

Direction 4

Tonhalle

black #0E0E0Ebone #F2F0EAgrey 1 #B8B6AFgrey 2 #8E8C86vermilion #E8452C

Swiss concert posters (Müller-Brockmann's Tonhalle series above all) proved that music can be shown with nothing but a grid, a grotesque and rhythm. Tonhalle applies that discipline to SetID on a black ground. The home headline is the comment-section question at poster scale, and the proof is the whole set drawn as rhythm: 21 lines, one per identified track, placed at the minute it played. No cards, no boxes, no dividers. Rank is brightness: the current track is white and vermilion, everything else steps back to grey. Listeners get one huge answer; DJs get a precise, quiet table they can read in a dark room. Schibsted Grotesk throughout, from 13 px table figures to 160 px display.

The set drawn as a rhythm graphic of 21 lines; rank expressed only as brightness

None on load. State changes are instant brightness steps

Direction 5

White Label

kraft #C49A6Csleeve #F7F4EEink #1B1712stamp green #0E6B43sticker #FF7A1Abiro #1E3BB3

Every DJ learned to dig in a record shop: flicking through a crate, reading the stamped divider card, trusting the handwritten staff pick taped to the rack. White Label turns SetID into that shop, and lets the real artwork do the talking. The home is a crate of actual covers from the set; the timeline is a strip of sleeves, each as wide as the time it played; similar tracks arrive as a staff-pick card in blue biro. Listeners recognise a cover faster than a title, and DJs get the digging ritual they already love. Bricolage Grotesque in its condensed cut for headlines and stamps, Kalam only for the handwritten staff pick. Kraft card, sleeve white, rubber-stamp green, price-gun orange.

Fonts
Bricolage Grotesque (opsz and width axes) + Kalam for the staff pick only
Palette
Committed kraft: kraft, sleeve white, ink, stamp green, sticker orange, biro blue
Signature
Real covers everywhere: the crate on the home, the sleeve strip as the timeline
Motion
The front record lifts out of the crate on hover; nothing moves on its own
Kept from declined challengers
Character catalog: every record carries fixed furniture in the same place (stamp time, sleeve, sticker BPM).
Honest risk
The most familiar of the five. Record-shop nostalgia is where many music products land.

How these five were chosen

  • Set Times leads.From a ranked list of seven worlds DJs and ravers know by heart (record-shop white labels, riso club flyers, cassette J-cards, festival set-times boards, Teletext music pages, Swiss concert posters, club ephemera), the roll put the set-times board first, so it leads instead of the most obvious pick.
  • White Label is the safest strong option.It topped the list, and it carries an honest familiarity risk, noted on its card.
  • Blend came from outside the list.A dealt challenger (a silk canopy whose overlaps make colour chords) held on product clarity: crossings are exactly where track IDs get asked. It stays a full direction.
  • Riso and Tonhalle come from the listand cover the drenched and dark, typographic ends of the brief.
  • Four challengers were declined, each leaving one discipline behind.Brick instructions: one module (Set Times). Night instruments: readings carry their last result (Set Times). Cracktro scroller: rank by brightness, no boxes (Tonhalle). Character catalog: fixed furniture per item (White Label). Green-field capsules: one colour owns the viewport (Riso).
